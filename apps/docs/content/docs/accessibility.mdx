title: Accessibility
description: What every Manicat UI component guarantees, how focus and motion work, and how to test your own screens.
---

Accessibility is part of each component's contract, not an add-on. This page lists what you can rely on and what
stays your job.

## What each component does

- **Keyboard first.** Every interactive component works without a pointer. Menus and listboxes follow the
  WAI-ARIA keyboard model: arrows to move, Enter or Space to choose, Escape to close, Home and End to jump.
- **Roles and state.** Controls expose `role`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-selected`, `aria-checked` or `aria-busy` as
  their state changes, and overlays label themselves with a `title`/`label` you must provide.
- **Focus is managed.** Dialogs, drawers and sheets trap focus while open and return it to the trigger on close.
  A loading button stays focusable, so keyboard users do not lose their place.
- **Names are required.** Inputs take a `label` prop, and icon-only controls take an accessible name. Components
  whose visible label would be redundant accept `aria-label` instead.
- **Status is not color alone.** Success, warning and danger always come with text or an icon.

## Focus without rings

Manicat UI does not draw an outline on focus. Focus is answered by the surface and the label: a field's border and
background change, a button's fill shifts. This keeps screens quiet, but it makes contrast your responsibility if
you restyle a component. When you override colors, keep a visible difference between the resting and focused
states.

## Motion

Every animation has a reduced motion branch. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, movement and blur are replaced
by short fades, and nothing important is communicated by motion alone. See [Motion](/docs/motion).

## Contrast

Text tokens are tuned for contrast in both themes: `--foreground` and `--text-secondary` meet WCAG AA on
`--background` and `--surface`. `--text-muted` is for hints and captions at 12px or larger only. If you change a
token, check the pair again.

## Touch targets

Controls are at least 36px tall (`--control-height-sm`) and default to 44px. Keep that when you build your own
layouts around them, and leave 8px between adjacent targets on touch devices.

## Testing your screens

1. Unplug the mouse and complete the main flow with Tab, arrows, Enter and Escape.
2. Turn on a screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA) and listen to one dialog and one form.
3. Enable *Reduce motion* in your operating system and reload.
4. Switch to dark mode and to 200% zoom.

If a component fails any of these, that is a bug. Open an issue with the item id and the steps.
